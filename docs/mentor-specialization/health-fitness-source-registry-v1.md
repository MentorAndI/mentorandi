# Health & Fitness Mentor - Source Registry v1

Purpose: curated source registry for the Health & Fitness Mentor. These sources support periodic refresh and provenance. They are not intended for live browsing on every user turn.

## Runtime rule

Default behavior should use internal techniques and knowledge cards. Live source lookup should be rare and reserved for periodic refresh, safety checks, or explicit product-reviewed retrieval.

## Safety boundaries

Do not use these sources to:

- diagnose conditions
- prescribe medical treatment
- prescribe injury rehabilitation
- advise on eating disorders
- prescribe supplements or medication
- make extreme weight-loss plans
- promise specific medical/body outcomes

Escalate to professional advice for medical symptoms, severe pain, suspected disordered eating, pregnancy-specific concerns, or complex health conditions.

## 1. WHO - Physical activity

URL: https://www.who.int/initiatives/behealthy/physical-activity

Authority: official public health organization

Use for:

- general physical activity framing
- adult movement baselines
- muscle-strengthening emphasis
- sedentary behavior context

Do not use for:

- individualized medical exercise prescription
- rehab plans
- high-performance programming

Summary:

WHO provides general public-health guidance that adults should do regular aerobic activity and muscle-strengthening activities. Use this as broad direction, not rigid individualized programming.

Refresh interval: quarterly

## 2. WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour

URL: https://www.who.int/europe/publications/i/item/9789240014886

Authority: official public health guideline

Use for:

- public-health baseline for movement
- sedentary behavior framing
- broad safety and wellbeing context

Do not use for:

WHO guideline summary supports the idea that some activity is better than none and that adults benefit from aerobic and strengthening activity. Use for high-level principles.

## 4. NHS - Eating a balanced diet

Authority: public health / national health service

Use for:

- balanced diet basics
- fruit and vegetables
- starchy carbohydrates
- protein and dairy/alternatives
- non-extreme food guidance

Do not use for:

- individualized diet plans for disease
- eating disorder support
- extreme restriction

NHS Eatwell framing is useful for simple, balanced, non-fad food guidance. Use it to keep the mentor's nutrition advice moderate and safe.

Refresh interval: quarterly
