# Parenting Support Mentor Source Registry v1

Purpose: curated source registry for Parenting Mentor. These sources are used for provenance, safety boundaries, periodic refresh, and internal knowledge-card improvement. They are not meant for live browsing on every user message.

Runtime rule: ordinary Parenting Mentor responses should use prebuilt technique and knowledge cards. Live lookup should be rare and limited to approved sources when freshness matters.

Refresh cadence: quarterly review, or sooner if safety/health guidance changes.

## Source 1: CDC Child Development and Positive Parenting Tips

URL: https://www.cdc.gov/child-development/
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/child-development/positive-parenting-tips/index.html

Authority: official public health source.

Use for:
- age-stage parenting framing
- developmental milestones awareness
- positive parenting basics
- safety boundary language
- parent-facing general guidance

Do not use for:
- diagnosis
- individualized medical advice
- replacing pediatric consultation

Internal summary:
CDC provides child development and positive parenting resources across life stages, including milestones, safety, health, and parent support. Use this as a conservative, high-trust reference for age-aware parenting content.

## Source 2: AAP HealthyChildren

URL: https://www.healthychildren.org/

Authority: American Academy of Pediatrics parent-facing information site.

Use for:
- pediatrician-aligned parenting basics
- child health and development framing
- screen time, sleep, family routines, safety, and behavior topics
- when to seek pediatric/professional help

Do not use for:
- direct medical diagnosis
- emergency handling beyond directing to urgent care/emergency services

Internal summary:
HealthyChildren is a practical parent-facing resource from the American Academy of Pediatrics. Use it to keep parenting guidance aligned with pediatric safety and development framing.

## Source 3: UNICEF Parenting

URL: https://www.unicef.org/parenting/

Authority: international child-focused organization.

Use for:
- accessible parenting tips
- emotional support for parents
- child care, bonding, play, and family communication
- global and culturally broad parent-facing language

Do not use for:
- diagnosis
- country-specific legal advice

Internal summary:
UNICEF Parenting provides practical, accessible parenting guidance and child-care resources. Use it for warm, non-shaming, child-centered parent support.

## Source 4: NSPCC Parenting Support and Child Safety

URL: https://www.nspcc.org.uk/keeping-children-safe/support-for-parents/

Authority: child protection charity and safety-focused resource.

Use for:
- child safety boundaries
- when ordinary parenting support is not enough
- abuse, neglect, or safeguarding escalation framing

Do not use for:
- ordinary family disagreement as if it were automatically abuse
- legal advice

Internal summary:
NSPCC is a useful safety and safeguarding reference. Use when the mentor needs to distinguish ordinary parenting stress from possible harm or risk.

## Source 5: NHS mental wellbeing / family wellbeing resources

URL: https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/

Authority: public health source.

Use for:
- parent wellbeing
- stress and emotional strain
- support-seeking language
- practical wellbeing guidance

Do not use for:
- child diagnosis
- medical decisions

Internal summary:
NHS Every Mind Matters can support parent wellbeing, stress, and self-help framing, especially when the parent is overwhelmed.

## General source rules

The Parenting Mentor should not cite sources to the user in ordinary replies unless the user asks for references or the answer relies on current/safety-specific guidance.

The source registry should inform:
- knowledge cards
- safety boundaries
- eval scenarios
- future admin review
- periodic refresh

Do not turn the mentor into a medical, legal, or child protection authority. When risk is high, direct the user to appropriate local emergency, medical, or safeguarding support.
